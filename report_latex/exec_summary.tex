%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section*{Headline}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

When a scientific concept enters a new subfield, the share of its initial co-occurrence partners that are native to that host predicts whether the concept will be taken up by the host's own newcomer authors.
The effect replicates on a held-out concept fold (IRR/SD 1.19 [1.06, 1.33], $p = 0.004$) and an independent MeSH biomedical population (IRR/SD 1.23 [1.12, 1.36], Holm $p < 0.001$), pools to IRR/SD 1.26 [1.17, 1.36], operates through both extensive and intensive margins, and is host-specific rather than reflecting general concept accessibility.

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section*{Goal}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

The study investigates two questions using an OpenAlex-based scholarly dataset of 426 semantically grounded concepts with 462,812 works:

\begin{description}
\item[RQ1 (Emergence)] Which temporal and structural patterns in an evolving, semantically grounded scientific knowledge graph characterise the emergence of scientific concepts?
\item[RQ2 (Diffusion)] How do emerging scientific concepts diffuse across disciplinary communities over time, and which temporal network patterns distinguish locally concentrated concepts from broadly integrated ones?
\end{description}

The target venue is \emph{Applied Network Science}.

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section*{What Was Tried}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

Five iterations over 25 artifacts:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Data foundation (Iteration~1).} Built a concept pool of 426 concepts (366 main, 60 reference), 462,812 works, and 488,078 verified concept-work links from OpenAlex with a held-out MeSH confirmation population of 191 biomedical concepts. Pre-registered all decision rules and hashed specifications before data inspection.

\item \textbf{Gates, viability, and emergence event study (Iteration~2).} Tested the citation-layer gate (Gate~A) at edge level; built 25 yearly co-word network snapshots ($\sim$27,000 nodes, $\sim$84,000 edges); ran the RQ1 precursor event study on the screen fold and MeSH population. Originally planned source-sink viability estimation; blocked by Gate~A failure.

\item \textbf{Deepen closure, test grafting, describe diffusion (Iteration~3).} Deepened the closure finding with turnover-proof openness measures. Tested host-entry grafting on 1,740 screen-fold entries using PPML regression. Built a $k{=}2$ diffusion typology and tested expansion-before-diffusion ordering.

\item \textbf{Held-out confirmation and replication (Iteration~4).} Opened four sealed held-out folds. Confirmed the co-primary grafting specification. Replicated on MeSH. Tested single-paper artefact, vocabulary decomposition, classifier circularity, closure-anchoring link, and adopter-level mechanism.

\item \textbf{Post-confirmation decomposition, audit, positioning (Iteration~5).} Ran three K-tests: margin decomposition (both extensive and intensive confirmed), host-specificity (confirmed), and field boundary (no variation). Full numerical audit (319 curated numbers, 99.4\% accuracy). Positioning dossier grading novelty against 25 candidates.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{What Failed}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Gate~A (citation-layer feasibility).} Only 17.3\% of host edge-years meet the within-host tracing threshold (vs 64.2\% under lenient any-parent tracing). The viability decomposition central to the original hypothesis could not be executed.
\item \textbf{Primary closure (R1\_DEAD).} The general closure deficit does not survive held-out confirmation (Holm $p = 0.147$). Only the persistent-neighbour variant survives.
\item \textbf{Breadth prediction.} Openness does not predict where concepts travel beyond growth baselines.
\item \textbf{Community roles.} Leiden community roles (BRIDGE, STAYER, MIGRANT) do not predict host-subfield entry (BRIDGE OR 0.64 [0.38, 1.08]).
\item \textbf{Co-transfer.} Arriving with origin companions is null across all folds and populations.
\item \textbf{Concept pool field coverage.} 363 of 366 eligible candidates from the arXiv arm; social sciences and humanities are absent.
\end{itemize}

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section*{Open Questions}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Generalisability.} The population is dominated by physics/CS arXiv concepts. Extension to social sciences and humanities is untested.
\item \textbf{Primary specification power.} The fully-saturated primary specification is underpowered on the held-out fold and MeSH. Only the co-primary specification supports the grafting claim.
\item \textbf{Establishment threshold null.} The binary establishment threshold ($\mathrm{EST}_{\mathrm{bin}}$) is null on the held-out fold ($+$2.54 pp, $p = 0.16$).
\item \textbf{Single-paper dominance.} 87\% of screen co-primary events are single-paper entries; the held-out interaction ($p = 0.004$) suggests the effect is stronger for single-paper entries.
\item \textbf{Adopter-vs-entry paradox.} The adopter-level vocabulary gradient (FOREIGN $>$ NATIVE) runs opposite to the entry-level finding (host-native partners predict uptake). Whether these reflect different mechanisms at different scales is unresolved.
\item \textbf{Audit residuals.} 292 numbers in artifact outputs were not transcribed into the report. Seeded-perturbation recall is 0.60.
\end{enumerate}
