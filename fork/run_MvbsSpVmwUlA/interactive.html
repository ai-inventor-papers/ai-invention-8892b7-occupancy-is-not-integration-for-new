The finding

The question. When a scientific concept first shows up in a subfield outside its origin, what decides whether researchers in that subfield take it up?

Try it: call an entry host-leaning above a threshold

The model uses the share as a continuous number. What if you split entries at a cut-off instead? Drag the threshold and the page recomputes, from the recorded entries, how often each side attracted any newcomer and how many newcomer papers it drew.

Raw comparison with no controls. The paper's model does not use a cut-off: only 1.1% of partner tags have a host share above 50%, which is why the continuous share is primary, and binary nativeness cut-offs at 0.5 and 0.7 are null. A PPML with concept, year and host fixed effects cannot be refitted honestly in a browser, so this view sweeps a threshold over the recorded outcomes instead.

    What the paper does not show

    • The strict model is inconclusive on held-out. With concept-by-year and host-by-year fixed effects, the held-out IRR is 0.98 (95% CI 0.70 to 1.37) on 250 of 972 events and 30 clusters, declared underpowered in advance. The held-out confirmation rests on the co-primary specification.
    • Graded, not a threshold. Binary establishment (at least three newcomer papers, with host papers in at least three of the five years) is null on the held-out fold (p = 0.165).
    • Population. The pool is dominated by physics and computer-science concepts from arXiv. The MeSH replication covers biomedicine-to-biomedicine entries only. Social sciences, humanities and engineering are untested.
    • What the held-out fold protects. Its outcomes were sealed and opened once, but its entry events, pre-entry features and size came from the same pipeline, and an early experiment computed origin-series summaries for 61 of its concepts.
    • Single-paper entries. 87% of co-primary Screen events involve a single entry-year paper. The pooled multi-team effect is positive (IRR 1.28, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.48); the held-out fold alone is underpowered for this test.
    • Inference. The cluster-robust variance estimator rejects 10% to 11% of null shuffles in simulation (target 5%). Wild-cluster bootstrap and permutation tests agree with the held-out result (wild p = 0.012, permutation p = 0.026).
    • Prediction. Adding host-vocabulary share lowers the held-out fold's out-of-sample mean deviance by 0.50, but the 95% CI of the change (−1.24 to +0.02) includes zero; on MeSH, out-of-fold deviance falls by 0.146 (95% CI of the change −0.306 to −0.008). The entry predictions above are descriptive.
    • Host-specificity. The Balassa-index lift measure has a within-fixed-effect correlation of 0.997 with the log of the continuous share, so it confirms the share rather than adding independent evidence.
    • Adopters. Adopters are enriched for prior exposure to the entry partners (OR 3.09; prevalence 0.79 vs. 0.61), on the Screen fold only. The design cannot separate absorptive capacity from topical proximity.
    • Closure. The one confirmed closure row had a screen event-study interval that included zero, 17 of 25 held-out controls are screen concepts, and covariate balance is poor.
