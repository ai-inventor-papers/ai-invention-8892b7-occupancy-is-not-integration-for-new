The finding

The question. When a scientific concept first appears in a subfield that is not its home, what decides whether researchers in that field take it up?

The study follows every of 426 emerging concepts across 462,812 OpenAlex works: the first year a concept appears in a non-origin subfield with at least five co-occurring partner terms. For each entry it measures the of those partners and counts the papers written about the concept in that subfield by over the next five years.

The answer. Entries whose first partners already belong to the host's vocabulary draw more newcomers. On the independent , a one-standard-deviation higher share raises newcomer uptake by 23% (pre-registered and replicated). Development-set estimates on the and folds agree. The competing idea, that concepts root when they arrive with their origin companions (), is null everywhere.

Does it hold on every population? Each row is a pre-registered estimate: the per SD of the regressor. The dashed line at 1 means no effect.

What the paper does not show

  • The main-arm folds are not true held-out tests. Event counts, population sizes and gating decisions were computed on both Screen and Held-out folds before outcomes were read. The independent evidence is the MeSH replication.
  • The stricter specification is underpowered. With concept-by-year and host-by-year fixed effects the Held-out fold keeps 250 of 972 events in 30 clusters and gives IRR 0.98 (95% CI 0.70 to 1.37). Switch the headline chart to “Primary FE” to see it.
  • The population is narrow. The pool is dominated by physics and computer-science concepts from arXiv, and MeSH covers only biomedicine-to-biomedicine entries. Social sciences, humanities and engineering are untested.
  • Binary rooting is null on the Held-out fold (p = 0.16). The effect is graded, not a threshold; the margin ladder above shows it fade at higher thresholds there.
  • Most entries are single papers. 87% of co-primary Screen events involve one entry-year paper. The pooled multi-team effect is positive (IRR 1.28, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.48), but the Held-out fold alone is underpowered for it.
  • Cluster-robust p-values run hot. The estimator rejects 10% to 11% of null shuffles (target 5%). Wild-cluster bootstrap and permutation tests agree with the headline (Held-out wild p = 0.012, permutation p = 0.026).
  • Host-specificity rests on a near-duplicate measure. The Balassa-index lift correlates 0.997 with the log of the continuous share within fixed effects, so it confirms the share rather than adding independent evidence.
  • The adopter-level result is Screen-only. Adopters are enriched for prior exposure to the entry partners (OR 3.09, 95% CI 2.32 to 4.28), most of all to origin-vocabulary partners (FOREIGN OR 2.88, ADJACENT 1.91, NATIVE 1.47). The design cannot separate absorptive capacity from topical proximity.
