The finding

When a scientific concept first shows up in a new subfield, most such entries fade. Which ones take root?

The paper looks at the first papers that carry a concept into a non-origin subfield, a , and asks what share of the concept's co-occurring partner terms already belong to the host's own vocabulary. That predicts how many papers in the host write about the concept over the next five years.

What the paper does not show

  • The main-arm folds are development sets. Event counts, population sizes and gating decisions were computed on both the Screen and Held-out folds before grafting outcomes were read. The independent evidence comes from the MeSH replication.
  • Population bias. The concept pool is dominated by physics, physical-sciences and computer-science concepts from arXiv. The MeSH population covers only biomedicine-to-biomedicine entries. Social sciences, humanities and engineering are untested.
  • Single-paper entries. 87% of co-primary Screen events involve a single entry-year paper. The pooled multi-team effect is positive (IRR 1.28, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.48), but the Held-out fold alone is underpowered for this test.
  • Cluster-robust inference over-rejects. The cluster-robust variance estimator rejects 10% to 11% of null shuffles in simulation (target 5%). Wild-cluster bootstrap and permutation tests agree on the Held-out fold (wild p = 0.012, permutation p = 0.026).
  • Host-specificity is only partly tested. The Balassa-index lift measure correlates 0.997 with the log of the host-vocabulary share within fixed effects, so it confirms the continuous measure rather than adding independent evidence.
  • Adopter evidence is Screen-only. The enrichment for prior partner exposure (OR 3.09) is not independently confirmed, and the design cannot separate absorptive capacity from topical proximity.
  • Closure on the Held-out fold. Matching falls back to Screen controls for 17 of 25 unique controls, and the MeSH closure result is null under the primary label.
