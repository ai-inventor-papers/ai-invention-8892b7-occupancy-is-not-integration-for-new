Host vocabulary predicts whether new scientific concepts take root across disciplinary boundaries

When a scientific concept crosses into a new disciplinary field, does it take root or vanish? This study tracks 426 emerging concepts across 462,812 publications and finds that the vocabulary composition at the moment of entry predicts the outcome. If the concept's initial partner terms are drawn from the host field's own vocabulary, newcomer scientists are more likely to adopt it over the following five years. On an independent population of 191 biomedical concepts, a one-standard-deviation increase in host-vocabulary share raised newcomer uptake by 23% (incidence-rate ratio 1.23, 95% CI 1.12 to 1.36). Co-transfer of origin companions is null: what matters is not importing the concept as a package, but connecting it to the host's existing language.

Method

The study proceeds in four stages. First, an outcome-blind pool of 426 emerging scientific concepts was assembled from OpenAlex, split into a screen fold (247 concepts) and a held-out fold (119), alongside an independent check population of 191 MeSH biomedical concepts. All are drawn from 462,812 OpenAlex works. Concepts are noun-phrase surface forms that first appeared in OpenAlex titles and abstracts between 2005 and 2016. A logistic-regression classifier (F1 = 0.82, AUC = 0.89) and a variant merger (B-cubed F1 = 0.78) were applied to ground every concept.

Second, 25 yearly three-year-window co-occurrence snapshots (approximately 27,000 nodes and 84,000 edges per snapshot) were built from a design-weighted background sample of 259,716 works, with association-strength edge weights and Leiden community detection. A matched event study then compared closure measures between concepts showing sustained uptake and matched controls in the pre-onset window.

Third, host-entry events were defined: the first year a concept appears in a non-origin subfield with at least five co-occurring partner terms. The host-vocabulary share, the tag-weighted mean of each partner's pre-entry publication share in the host subfield, was the exposure variable. A Poisson pseudo-maximum-likelihood (PPML) regression with concept-clustered standard errors tested whether this share predicts five-year newcomer uptake by author-disjoint newcomers. Two specifications were pre-registered: a co-primary (concept, entry-year and host fixed effects) and a primary (concept-by-year and host-by-year fixed effects).

Fourth, the effect was decomposed into extensive and intensive margins, tested for host-specificity against generality controls, and examined at the adopter level through conditional logistic regression on prior partner exposure.

Results

Structural precursors of emergence (RQ1)

On the Held-out fold (a development-set estimate), only persistent-neighbour closure, restricted to top-20 neighbours present in consecutive years, survives Holm correction. General closure is not significant (Holm p = 0.147), and turnover-residualised closure is also null (Holm p = 0.635). Burt constraint is positive (+0.11, 95% CI 0.02 to 0.19), meaning that emerging concepts sit in ego networks that are more constrained, not less: the lower closure before emergence is not Burt brokerage.

Mechanism

The host-vocabulary effect operates through both the extensive margin (whether any newcomer uptake starts) and the intensive margin (the count of newcomer papers given that uptake started). The extensive margin accounts for 38% of the total effect (95% CI 21% to 55%), and the intensive margin for the remaining 62%.

Adding generality controls (subfield entropy and log frequency of entry partners) retains 97% of the host-vocabulary coefficient. The Wald test for equality across origin-field groups is null (p = 0.64): the effect does not vary by origin field.

At the adopter level, authors who take up a concept are enriched for prior exposure to its entry partners (conditional logistic regression OR 3.09; prevalence 0.79 vs. 0.61; 1,013 matched strata, 422 entries, 109 concepts; Screen fold, not independently confirmed).

Limitations

  • Development-set estimates. The Screen and Held-out folds were both used during pipeline development: event counts, population sizes and gating decisions were computed on both folds before grafting outcomes were read. Estimates on both main-arm folds are development-set estimates, not true held-out confirmations. The independent evidence for the host-vocabulary effect comes from the MeSH replication.
  • Population bias. The concept pool is dominated by physics, physical-sciences and computer-science concepts from arXiv. The MeSH population covers only biomedicine-to-biomedicine entries. Generalisation to social sciences, humanities or engineering is untested.
  • Primary specification underpowered. The fully saturated primary specification with concept-by-year and host-by-year fixed effects is inconclusive on the Held-out fold (IRR 0.98, 95% CI 0.70 to 1.37, 30 clusters). The independent MeSH replication and the development-set estimates rest on the co-primary specification, which leaves more residual variation.
  • Single-paper entries. 87% of co-primary Screen events involve a single entry-year paper.
  • Binary establishment null. The host-vocabulary share predicts the count of newcomer papers and the probability that any newcomer uptake occurs, but binary establishment (at least five newcomer papers in at least three of five years) is null on the Held-out fold (p = 0.16). The effect is graded, not threshold-based.
  • Cluster-robust inference. The cluster-robust variance estimator rejects 10% to 11% of null shuffles in simulation (target 5%). Wild-cluster bootstrap and permutation tests give consistent results, but the standard p-values should not be taken at face value.
  • Host-specificity caveat. The Balassa-index lift measure has a within-fixed-effect correlation of 0.997 with the log of the continuous host-vocabulary share. The lift test confirms the continuous measure rather than providing independent evidence for host-specificity.
  • Adopter evidence. The adopter-level enrichment (OR 3.09) is Screen-fold only, not independently confirmed. The design cannot separate absorptive capacity from topical proximity.
  • Closure caveats. The Held-out closure result is a development-set estimate. The Held-out matching falls back to Screen controls for 17 of 25 unique controls. The MeSH closure result is directional under the sensitivity label but null under the primary label.
