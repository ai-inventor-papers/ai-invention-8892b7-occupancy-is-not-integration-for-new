Host vocabulary predicts whether new scientific concepts take root across disciplinary boundaries

What determines whether a scientific concept takes root when it first enters a new field? This study of 426 OpenAlex concepts (366 emerging), 462,812 works and 191 new MeSH biomedical concepts finds that the vocabulary composition of a concept's initial partners in a host subfield predicts five-year newcomer uptake. Each standard deviation of host-vocabulary share raises newcomer uptake by 30% on the screen fold (incidence-rate ratio 1.30, 95% CI 1.16 to 1.45), by 19% on a sealed held-out fold opened once (1.19, 1.06 to 1.33) and by 23% in the MeSH population (1.23, 1.12 to 1.36; biomedicine-to-biomedicine entries only). Co-transfer of origin companions has no effect. Structural precursors of emergence turn out to be volume/churn correlates.

Method

Data

The study assembled an outcome-blind pool of 426 scientific concepts from OpenAlex, where outcome-blind means that inclusion criteria were frozen and SHA-256-hashed before any post-appearance data were inspected (code). Concepts are noun-phrase surface forms that first appeared in OpenAlex titles and abstracts between 2005 and 2016, with 20 to 300 papers in the first-appearance window. Of 426, 366 are main emerging concepts (247 Screen, 119 Held-out) and 60 are stationary reference. A logistic-regression classifier (F1 = 0.82, AUC = 0.89) and variant merger (B-cubed F1 = 0.78) ground every concept (code). An independent biomedical arm of 191 concepts drawn from new MeSH descriptors provides a check population. The combined corpus comprises 462,812 unique OpenAlex works.

From a design-weighted background sample of 259,716 works, 25 yearly three-year-window co-occurrence snapshots (approximately 27,000 nodes and 84,000 edges per snapshot) were built with association-strength edge weights and Leiden community detection (code).

RQ1: Structural precursors of emergence

The pre-declared emergence label (at least 20 papers per year and a gain of at least 20 percentile points in co-word network strength) produced only 4 onsets, so a sustained-uptake label (E_up) was promoted post hoc and is used for every RQ1 result; its held-out test was frozen before the held-out fold was opened. For each such concept, a matched control is drawn from the same first-appearance band and origin field via 1:1 nearest-neighbour matching on pre-period network indicators. The event-study estimand is the standardised mean difference between treated and control concepts at pre-onset lags. Four closure variants are tested: general closure, turnover-residualised closure, persistent-neighbour closure (restricted to top-20 neighbours present in consecutive years), and Burt constraint. Bootstrap confidence intervals (B = 2,000) and Holm-corrected p-values control for multiple testing.

RQ2: Host-entry grafting

A host-entry event is the first year a concept appears in a non-origin subfield with at least five co-occurring partner terms. Host-vocabulary share is the tag-weighted mean of each partner's pre-entry publication share in the host subfield. The outcome is five-year newcomer uptake: the count of host-subfield papers by author-disjoint newcomers during the five years after entry. The model is Poisson pseudo-maximum-likelihood (PPML) regression with concept-clustered standard errors. Two specifications were pre-registered: the primary (concept-by-year and host-by-year fixed effects) and the co-primary (concept, entry-year and host fixed effects), with co-transfer as a second regressor (code).

Results

Structural precursors of emergence (RQ1)

On the sealed held-out fold, general closure is not significant (Holm p = 0.147), and the frozen kill mapping returns R1_DEAD: structural precursors of sustained uptake are volume/churn correlates. Only persistent-neighbour closure, restricted to top-20 neighbours present in consecutive years, survives Holm correction: panel coefficient −1.07 (Holm p = 0.015), matched event-study standardised mean difference −0.83 (95% CI [−1.71, −0.10]). Turnover-residualised closure also fails (Holm p = 0.635). The persistent-neighbour row is a qualified lead: its screen event-study interval included zero, 17 of 25 held-out controls are screen concepts, and covariate balance is poor.

Burt constraint is positive (+0.11, 95% CI [0.02, 0.19]): emerging concepts sit in ego networks that are more constrained, not less. The lower closure before emergence is not Burt brokerage. The picture is of open, fast-renewing connections among a concept's most strongly associated terms within a dense wider ego network.

Structural precursors add no predictive value for emergence beyond frequency, burst, degree and entropy baselines (logistic AUC 0.89 vs. 0.88). The MeSH result is directional under the sensitivity label (D = −0.42, Holm p = 0.039, n = 29) but null under the primary label (D = −0.09, Holm p = 0.677, n = 15) (code).

Host-entry grafting (RQ2)

On the independent MeSH biomedical population, a one-standard-deviation increase in host-vocabulary share raises newcomer uptake by 23% (co-primary IRR 1.23, 95% CI 1.12 to 1.36, p < 0.001; primary IRR 1.32, 95% CI 1.10 to 1.60, p = 0.004; pre-registered verdict: REPLICATED) (code).

On the main-arm folds: Screen co-primary IRR 1.30 (95% CI 1.16 to 1.45); on the sealed held-out fold, opened once, co-primary IRR 1.19 (95% CI 1.06 to 1.33, Holm p = 0.009, wild-cluster bootstrap p = 0.012, nativeness-permutation p = 0.026), so the pre-declared kill rule is not triggered (code). The inverse-variance weighted pool of the Screen and MeSH co-primary estimates is 1.26 (95% CI 1.17 to 1.36, I² = 0). Co-transfer is null across all folds and specifications.

The fully saturated primary specification on the Held-out fold is inconclusive (IRR 0.98, 95% CI 0.70 to 1.37, 30 clusters, p = 0.91) because it retains only 250 of 972 events; this was declared underpowered in advance (minimum detectable effect IRR 1.30).

The co-primary effect holds in 20 of 26 robustness variants on the Screen fold (IRR range 1.11 to 1.56). Nativeness-permutation placebos are centred on 1.0 (co-primary p = 0.002 Screen, 0.026 Held-out, 0.010 MeSH).

Margin decomposition

The host-vocabulary effect operates through both the extensive margin (whether any newcomer uptake starts) and the intensive margin (the count of newcomer papers given that uptake started). The inverse-variance weighted extensive-margin effect is +6.4 percentage points per standard deviation (95% CI 4.8 to 8.1), approximately a 13% increase relative to the 51% base rate. The intensive-margin IRR is 1.18 (95% CI 1.10 to 1.27). The extensive share of the total effect is 0.38 (95% CI 0.21 to 0.55) (code).

Adding generality controls (subfield entropy and log frequency) retains 97% of the host-vocabulary coefficient (IVW retention 0.97, 95% CI 0.81 to 1.10). The Balassa-index lift measure is significant (IVW IRR 1.34, 95% CI 1.23 to 1.46) but near-collinear with the continuous share (within-FE correlation 0.997) (code).

Adopter-level mechanism

Newcomer authors who adopt a concept in its host subfield are enriched for prior corpus exposure to the concept's entry partners (conditional logistic regression OR 3.09; prevalence 0.79 vs. 0.61; 95% CI 2.32 to 4.28; 1,013 matched strata, 422 entries, 109 concepts) (code). Exposure to frequency-matched negative-control concepts is negatively associated (OR 0.62), and the placebo control using partners of a different concept's entry is also below 1 (OR 0.72), showing specificity. The result is consistent with absorptive capacity or with topical proximity; exposure is corpus-only, and 87% of adopter pairs have no prior corpus work. Enrichment does not mediate the entry-level effect. This is a Screen-fold result, not independently confirmed.

Null findings

Co-transfer of origin companions is null in all specifications. The breadth-prediction screen (does pre-emergence openness predict how many new subfields a concept will reach?) found no effect beyond growth and level baselines (code). The link between pre-emergence closure and later host-entry composition is null (partial correlation 0.025 Screen, 0.053 Held-out, both confidence intervals including zero): network openness and vocabulary composition operate independently.

Limitations

  • What the held-out fold protects. The held-out fold's outcomes were sealed and opened once, but its entry events, pre-entry features and population size were computed on the same pipeline before opening, so it guards against outcome-driven choices rather than every choice of frame. MeSH was used for the RQ1 replication before the grafting test.
  • Population bias. The concept pool is dominated by physics and computer-science concepts from arXiv. The MeSH population covers only biomedicine-to-biomedicine entries. Generalisation to social sciences, humanities or engineering is untested.
  • Primary specification underpowered. The fully saturated specification (concept-by-year and host-by-year fixed effects) is inconclusive on the Held-out fold (IRR 0.98, 30 clusters). The held-out confirmation rests on the co-primary specification; on MeSH the primary specification is significant (IRR 1.32).
  • Single-paper entries. 87% of co-primary Screen events involve a single entry-year paper. The pooled multi-team effect is positive (IRR 1.28, 95% CI 1.11 to 1.48), but the Held-out fold alone is underpowered for this test.
  • Binary establishment null. Host-vocabulary share predicts the count of newcomer papers and the probability that any uptake occurs, but binary establishment (at least three newcomer papers, with host papers in at least three of the five years) is null on the Held-out fold (p = 0.165). The effect is graded, not threshold-based.
  • Cluster-robust inference. The cluster-robust variance estimator rejects 10% to 11% of null shuffles in simulation (target 5%). Wild-cluster bootstrap and permutation tests give consistent results, but standard p-values should not be taken at face value.
  • Host-specificity caveat. The Balassa-index lift measure has a within-fixed-effect correlation of 0.997 with the log of the continuous host-vocabulary share. The lift test confirms the continuous measure rather than providing independent evidence.
  • Screen-fold adopter evidence. The adopter-level enrichment (OR 3.09) is Screen-fold only and not independently confirmed. The design cannot separate absorptive capacity from topical proximity.
  • Closure held-out caveats. RQ1 is R1_DEAD under the frozen kill rule; the one confirmed row, persistent-neighbour closure, had a screen event-study interval that included zero. The Held-out matching falls back to Screen controls for 17 of 25 unique controls. The MeSH closure result is directional under the sensitivity label but null under the primary label.
